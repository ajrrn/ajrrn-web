We are at a crucial juncture when it comes to border control and technology. Global displacement is at a historic high. States are increasingly reaching for new technologies in border control — biometric recognition systems, automated decision‑making tools, lie‑detection technologies, drones. At the same time, new technologies also offer promising opportunities for resistance.

The **Algorithmic Justice for Refugees Research Network (AJRRN)** is a global, interdisciplinary effort dedicated to safeguarding the rights of refugees and other people on the move through:

1. **critical investigation** of rights‑violating technological practices, and
2. **the development** of innovative, rights‑enhancing tools.

By simultaneously critiquing rights‑violating technologies and developing rights‑enhancing tools, the network fosters collaboration among scholars, practitioners and displaced people to increase knowledge and understanding of these technologies, influence policy, advance legal frameworks, and promote technological innovation that supports the rights of refugees and other people on the move.

## How we work

The network is guided by the principle **“nothing about us without us.”** Research on technology and border control must be shaped by those who experience its realities. Researchers with lived experience of displacement and community organizations take part in setting priorities, selecting projects and leading research — not only as participants, but as partners and co‑authors.

Our work is organized in two overlapping areas:

- **Human rights and digital border governance** uses human rights law to study and challenge technologies of border control.
- **Rights‑enhancing technologies** builds and evaluates open‑source tools that support refugees, their advocates and decision‑makers.

## Who we are

The AJRRN is hosted by the [Refugee Law Lab](https://refugeelab.ca) at Osgoode Hall Law School and the Centre for Refugee Studies, York University (Toronto, Canada). It brings together universities, legal and advocacy organizations, technology and digital‑rights groups, and researchers with lived experience of displacement from around the world.

## Our commitments

- **Open access.** Our publications, data and code are open whenever it is safe to make them so, and protected when necessary.
- **Multilingual.** Core materials are made available in English, French, Spanish and Arabic.
- **Equity.** We aim for research environments where naming and confronting oppression is welcomed, and where researchers from equity‑deserving groups — including people with migration experience — lead.
- **Care with data.** Our goal is not perfect data management, but thoughtful practices that respect the people whose lives and stories are reflected in our research.
