The Algorithmic Justice for Refugees Research Network (AJRRN) is funding four graduate-led research projects, one in each of the areas below. Each successful applicant receives $10,000 CAD to carry out the project and publish the results as an open access journal article.

**Applications close December 1, 2026, 11:59 pm Toronto time.**

## The four projects

Apply to one project only.

### Project 1 — Global Majority Data in Global North Border Control

Map how a Global Majority country or region is drawn into Global North border control through its data. The project reviews literature on border externalisation (including work on the externalisation of EU borders into the Sahel), digital ID and data colonialism; documents how data leaves the country or region; assesses those arrangements against human rights and data protection law; and makes recommendations.

**Outputs:** a published open access journal article, and possibly a public map or dataset. Preference for this project will be given to applicants from the Global Majority.

### Project 2 — Identifying Border Technology and Vendors in Canada

Develop and test methods for finding which border-control technologies the Canadian government buys and deploys, and who builds them. The project reviews existing research and approaches used in other jurisdictions, evaluates which methods are likely to work in Canada, and applies one method as a proof of concept.

**Output:** a published open access journal article.

### Project 3 — Scoping Rights-Enhancing Technologies

Establish what rights-enhancing technological tools already exist for refugees and other displaced people, their advocates and decision-makers. The project develops a definition and inclusion criteria for rights-enhancing tools, builds a preliminary catalogued inventory, and identifies gaps where new tools would add most value.

**Outputs:** a published open access journal article and an open inventory hosted by the network.

### Project 4 — LLM Bias Benchmark for Refugees and Migrants

Build and release a benchmark measuring how large language models treat refugees and migrants, and report results for several widely used models. The project defines the forms of bias that matter, designs the dataset, evaluates models and analyses implications for migration settings.

**Outputs:** a published open access journal article and an open-source benchmark (dataset, code and documentation).

## Who can apply

Current university graduate students, and postdoctoral fellows who do not hold tenure-stream faculty appointments, in Canada or abroad, in law, computer science, software engineering, data science or a related field. Eligibility is assessed at the time of application, so completing your degree during the project is fine.

Each applicant finds their own academic supervisor. Any full-time university faculty member qualifies; they do not have to be your graduate supervisor.

## Funding, authorship and outputs

- **Funding:** $10,000 CAD per project, paid in three equal tranches: at contract signing; when your supervisor confirms a working paper is ready to circulate; and when the article is accepted by an open access journal from the list agreed in your contract.
- **Authorship:** you are lead author. Your supervisor may co-author but is not required to.
- **Open licence:** the article, and any data or code, must be released under an open licence.

## How to apply

[Apply online](https://cryptpad.fr/form/#/2/form/view/vSOcwx2LFfbz5siUqCrycIXMLh4CKXZgJL8GAY1VDtM/embed/). Everything is typed directly into the form, with no file uploads. Please note that due to CryptPad’s setup, you will not receive an email to confirm your application is received. You will be asked for:

- **Project proposal** (the equivalent of 1 page) setting out your research question(s) and focus; your methods and why they suit the question; how you would meet the 6-month working paper and 12-month publication targets, including one or two open access journals you might submit to; and briefly, how your background prepares you for the work.
- **Initial bibliography** (the equivalent of 1 page).
- **CV** (the equivalent of 2 pages maximum) covering your education, relevant research, publications and experience.
- **Supervisor confirmation:** paste in your supervisor’s email confirming that they support your application. No letter of reference is needed.

The form also asks for your supervisor’s name, email, institution and institutional website biography. Optional questions let you share equity, diversity and inclusion factors you would like considered, including lived experience of migration.

## Privacy

AJRRN is hosted by the Refugee Law Lab at York University. Your information is used only to review applications and is seen only by those involved in selection.
